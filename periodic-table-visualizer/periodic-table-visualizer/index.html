Reading the table

Hydrogen through cadmium have complete explorers. Click or tap a tile for its card, then use Explore. Double-click is a mouse shortcut. Other elements have information cards only.

Keyboard: Tab moves between tiles. Arrow keys move to neighboring occupied positions. Enter selects. Escape closes the element card and returns focus to its tile.

Our conventions

Hydrogen is a reactive nonmetal, even though it sits in group 1. The detached rows contain La–Lu and Ac–Lr; their individual group numbers are omitted because group-3 conventions vary.

Families are useful teaching labels, not absolute boundaries. Here Zn, Cd and Hg share the broad d-block/transition-metal family; Po is a post-transition metal and At a halogen. The six metalloids are B, Si, Ge, As, Sb and Te. Elements 109–118 use “Unknown / uncertain properties” to avoid presenting predicted bulk behavior as measured fact. Their conventional groups remain visible.

Block labels follow the RSC convention, including d for La and Ac and f for Lu and Lr. Block and family are different labels. A filled shell or an exceptional electron configuration does not make a complete classification rule.

    About this model

Protons and neutrons have internal structure, which will be explored in a later phase. Copper-63 is one stable isotope of copper.

    Quick definitions
    Proton
    A positively charged particle in the nucleus.
    Neutron
    An electrically neutral particle in the nucleus.
    Electron
    A negatively charged particle described by quantum states around the nucleus.
    Element
    A substance whose atoms share the same proton count.
    Atomic number
    The number of protons; it identifies the element.
    Mass number
    Total protons and neutrons in this isotope, not the periodic table’s weighted average atomic mass.
    Ion
    An atom with a net charge caused by gaining or losing electrons.
    Isotope
    Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
    Valence electron
    An electron in the outer region involved in bonding; this model highlights copper’s single outer-shell electron.
