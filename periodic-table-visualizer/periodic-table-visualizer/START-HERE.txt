INTERACTIVE PERIODIC TABLE & ATOM EXPLORER — PHASE 6M — COMPLETE ACTINIDE ROW

1. Copy the entire periodic-table-visualizer folder to your computer or thumb drive.
2. Keep all files and subfolders together.
3. Open index.html in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari.
4. No internet connection, installation, or server is required.
5. If double-clicking does not work, right-click index.html, choose Open with,
   and select a modern browser. You can also use the browser's Open File command.
   If a school device blocks local pages, ask your teacher or IT administrator.

NEW: COMPLETE ACTINIDE ROW
All actinides Ac–Lr now open from the detached row. Cm–Lr add 13 radioactive
isotope choices, with tiny-quantity research uses and clear measurement limits.
Open Completing the Actinides to follow 5f filling, Cm's 6d exception, No's
filled 5f group, and Lr's unusual 7p electron. Lr has three electrons in shell 7.
Choose Proton, Neutron, Electron or Outer-region electron in the particle menu.
Double-click a proton or neutron to explore its simplified internal structure.
Lr defaults to Lr-266. Its [262] tile follows the established table convention;
neither bracketed value is an average atomic weight. Lr-266's reported half-life
is very uncertain. A long half-life does not mean a radioactive material is safe.
These are educational models, not instructions for handling radioactive materials.

PERIODIC TABLE HOME
Opening index.html shows all 118 elements. Search by name, symbol or exact
atomic number. Select family buttons to combine filters; clear them at any time.
Colors match the family legend and can be turned off. Tiles keep their positions.
Use Table for periodic relationships or List for an easy-to-scan alternative.
On smaller screens, scroll the table sideways. View details jumps to the
selected element card. Close or Escape returns focus to the selected tile.

Every element has basic information. Hydrogen through lawrencium have
complete explorers. Single-click or tap an element for its card, then use
Explore Atom to enter. Double-click an enabled tile with a mouse to enter
directly. Other elements still open their information cards. Tiles no longer
carry a 3D badge.
Touch users should use the Explore button; table double-tap is not a shortcut.
Back to Periodic Table preserves your
search, filters, selection, scroll and current explorer settings. Browser Back/Forward
works too. Reload retains the main view; temporary settings reset.
Direct local links use symbols, for example index.html#explore=He,
index.html#explore=Na, index.html#explore=Si or index.html#explore=Ar.
Use the isotope selector in Atom Profile to change the neutron count.
Returning to the same explorer retains its settings. Changing elements loads
the new default isotope and resets the camera to fit. Reload also resets
temporary settings. The folder name is unchanged.

TRY MERCURY THROUGH RADON
Open index.html#explore=Hg (or Tl, Pb, Bi, Po, At, Rn).
Defaults: Hg-202, Tl-205, Pb-208, Bi-209, Po-209, At-210 and Rn-222.
Use the Atom Profile dropdown for the 21 isotope choices in this group.
Bi-209 is radioactive but changes extraordinarily slowly. Po, At and Rn have
no stable isotopes. The bracketed table values are not average atomic weights.
Open Completing Period 6 to follow 6p from Tl1 to Rn6, after Hg's filled 5d10.
Descriptions distinguish one atom from mercury liquid or radon gas in bulk.
Read the Science and Electrical Engineering panel for age-appropriate uses
and safety context, including indoor radon testing and mitigation.
Navigation continues through all elements H–Lr and stops before Rf.
See PHASE-6L-REPORT.md for this phase's sources, tests and limitations.

TRY TERBIUM THROUGH LUTETIUM
The detached lanthanide row is now complete. Open index.html#explore=Tb,
#explore=Dy, #explore=Ho, #explore=Er, #explore=Tm, #explore=Yb or #explore=Lu.
Defaults: Tb-159, Dy-164, Ho-165, Er-166, Tm-169, Yb-174 and Lu-175.
Atom Profile offers 29 isotope choices across these seven elements.
The inner 4f group fills from nine electrons in Tb to fourteen in Yb.
Lu keeps fourteen 4f electrons and adds one 5d electron. The two outermost
6s electrons are not the entire bonding picture. Open Across the Lanthanides.
Lu-176 is naturally radioactive; Tb-161, Ho-166, Tm-170 and Lu-177 are
produced radioactive choices. Lu-177 medicines have established uses;
Tb-161 cancer treatments are described as research and clinical trials.
The full app now has 95 explorers and 383 isotope/nuclear-state records.
See PHASE-6I-REPORT.md for this phase's tests, sources and limitations.

Use the source and isotope guides to learn more.

TRY HYDROGEN
Hydrogen-1 (Protium) has 1 proton, no neutron and 1 electron.
Hydrogen-2 (Deuterium) has 1 proton, 1 neutron and 1 electron; it is stable.
Hydrogen-3 (Tritium) has 1 proton, 2 neutrons and 1 electron; it is radioactive
with a half-life of about 12.3 years. The picture does not simulate decay.
The isotope selector changes only the neutron count. Hydrogen-1's Neutron
option is disabled. Hydrogen's only electron is also its valence electron.
All three models work. Schrödinger shows only the occupied 1s cloud.
Natural Abundance and Cosmic Context explains variable Earth abundances and
their distinction from space. Verified sources are available in the profile.

not exact pictures or numerical simulations. See README.txt for details.

EXPLORE INSIDE A PROTON OR NEUTRON
Select Proton or Neutron, then press Explore Inside. You can also double-click
or double-tap a visible proton or neutron. A drag only rotates the picture.

Inside, choose Up quark, Down quark, Gluon field, or the whole particle.
Drag to turn, scroll/pinch to zoom, and use Pause or Reset as usual.
Back to Atom or Escape returns. Back to Nucleus returns at a closer atom zoom.
The explanation-level selector works inside too.

Full Screen presents the current view. Inside fullscreen, Back to Atom keeps
presentation open; Exit Full Screen returns the normal panels.

Proton: two up quarks and one down quark.
Neutron: one up quark and two down quarks.
The changing field shows a simplified strong interaction, not real footage.
The whole folder still opens offline through index.html.

PHASE 6F — INDIUM THROUGH XENON
In, Sn, Sb, Te, I and Xe complete Period 5. Their explorers remain available.
This earlier phase brought the total to 54 explorers and 230 records; the
current totals are 95 explorers and 383 records.

Choose isotopes in Atom Profile. Tin-117m has the same 50 protons and 67
neutrons as tin-117, but an excited nucleus. The m does not add a neutron.
Natural In-115, Te-128/130 and Xe-124/136 are labelled very long-lived
radioactive isotopes. Tin-117m and produced isotopes have no invented natural
percentages. Five shells, every electron and all eleven occupied subshells
remain available in the new elements. See PHASE-6F-REPORT.md for details.
